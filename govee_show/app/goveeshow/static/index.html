How to trace your lights

Take the photo from where people will watch, ideally at dusk. Then pick a light in the list, press Trace and click along where it runs on the photo, starting at the end with the plug. Click once at each corner or bend. Drag a point to move it, right-click a point to remove it, and press Enter or Done when the light is traced. Each light is shown in a rainbow from the plug (red) to the far end, so you can check its direction. The editor's House panel then plays the show on the photo.

Part of a light out of sight, like round the back of the house, or an extension lead? Trace past it anyway (a short line will do), then click that stretch and choose Out of view with how many bulbs are on it. Those bulbs still take their share of the segments but aren't drawn.

Use a palette from an effect's settings: click a colour to drop it into a colour field, or Apply palette to fill them all. Click a light's name to Recolour every effect on it, or on every light.

Automations

To play this show from an automation, use the Govee Light Show MQTT entities (pick the show, then press Play), or the hassio.addon_stdin action with input {"action": "play", "show": "…"}. The song plays on the show's Show speaker (in the editor's Song panel), or on the default below when a show doesn't pick one. The speaker in the Playback panel is only for editing, so you can design on a speaker indoors without changing where the real show plays. Speakers are added on the Speakers tab.

Layout

Choose where each editor panel sits: along the top or bottom, down the left or right, or in the middle. Choose Hidden to tuck a panel away and bring it back here. In the editor you can also drag a panel by its title to move it or change its order, or use the ⋯ button on it. The layout is saved in this browser.

Experimental

Adds a Govee scene effect that hands a light to one of the scenes built into the Govee app (the Scenes list, not your saved snapshots) for the length of the clip. It uses an unofficial command and Govee's public scene list, so it may not work on every light. The editor's preview can't show the scene itself, and the light may blink as it switches in and out.
